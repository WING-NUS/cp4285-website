Choose **one** production recommendation surface from the four approved cases below. Focus only on a **single** particular user-facing recommendation surface, rather than the company in general.

| Recommender Case | Example recommendation surface |
|---|---|
| **Grab** | Grab Feed cards, merchant/food discovery, offers, or cross-service recommendations |
| **Lazada** | Product discovery, category/search ranking, campaign product selection, or new-product exposure |
| **Instagram** | Explore, Reels, or Feed ranking |
| **LinkedIn** | LinkedIn Jobs personalised job recommendations, job alerts, or Hiring Assistant candidate sourcing |

For your selected recommendation surface, critically analyse and propose a technically credible recommender design for the particular recommendation aspect you have selected. Draw on the Week 01–02 material (Collaborative Filtering & Latent Factor Models) in constructing and defending your argument.  A strong argument accurately applies relevant Week 01–02 recommender concepts and explains why they are appropriate for the chosen recommendation surface.

The structure and emphasis of the essay are deliberately yours to determine. Your task is to make a focused technical argument, rather than to follow a fixed template. You should decide what evidence, explanation, comparison, and level of implementation detail are necessary to persuade an informed reader that the proposed approach is appropriate for the case.

You should not describe a **complete** production recommender system; rather, select one or two technical concepts most consequential to your chosen surface and develop a precise, evidence-based argument.

The case study should be grounded in the selected platform’s actual product context, but it is not an exercise in recovering a proprietary algorithm. Clearly distinguish **documented facts** (if any) about the platform from your own **reasoned technical proposal**. The work should engage seriously with algorithmic choice, the realities of operating a recommender over a large and changing catalogue, and the relationship between technical choices and inferred recommendation objectives.

You must also return to the **Week 01 slides** and select one of the card-suit archetypes to use as a substantive analytical lens. The selected lens must feature in your technical design choice, objective, constraint, feature, ranking rule, or evaluation criterion; merely defining the archetype is insufficient.

# AI Use, Peer Collaboration, Ownership, and Verification

**AI use is encouraged.** Use it as a research and thinking partner to identify vocabulary, locate possible lines of inquiry, explain unfamiliar production concepts, generate alternative technical designs, test counterarguments, and improve the clarity of your draft. It is not evidence, however, and all AI-generated claims and citations must be verified against the original sources.

You must **lead the investigation**. Before using AI substantively, establish the scaffolding for its work: identify the recommendation surface, set a provisional question or claim, state the technical issue you wish to investigate, and give the system a focused task. Your prompts should steer the inquiry rather than ask AI to produce an unexamined essay. The central idea, interpretation of the case, technical judgment, organisation, and final argument must be your own. AI may inform your thinking, but it must not replace the ideation that gives the essay its intellectual direction.

In an **Acknowledgements** section after the Reference List, attach an **AI-use declaration**. It must identify the tool(s) used; explain how you framed, constrained, or scaffolded the AI’s investigation; state how AI contributed; and give two examples of outputs that you checked, corrected, or rejected. Retain your relevant prompts and outputs in case they are requested.

Students are encouraged to discuss the general aspects concerning your recommender case to further your understanding of the course's topics.  Discussion may clarify concepts and test reasoning, but students must not co-author prose or submit materially shared technical arguments.  Discussions that substantially affected your architectural decisions should also be declared in your **Acknowledgements** section.  Note: the permission to discuss general course concepts does not apply to an allocated peer essay or to the preparation of your peer review.

Students may be asked to discuss or explain their essay, its sources, its technical choices, and their use of AI. This is an opportunity to demonstrate ownership of the work.
